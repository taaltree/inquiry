INQUIRY

A science game on a university campus · on foot, by bike, by question

A shooter where the weapon is a question. Walk a university campus, interview 24 real scientists about how they actually knew what they knew, then use what you learn as ammunition — teaching the students around you and dismantling the misinformation hunting them.

1

Ask

Five questions, one instrument. Ask all five of a researcher and you have reconstructed their reasoning, not memorised their result.

2

Teach

Fire an insight at a student and they take it. Educated students visibly change — and start defending themselves.

3

Dismantle

When a claim reaches you the world stops. No timer. Read it, name the technique, pick the one question that breaks it.

4

Secure

Finish a district's researchers, vault, students and feed to secure it. Secure all five to reach the Convocation.

What this is for, and what it is not

Learning outcomes

Evidence & inference

Separate a claim from the evidence for it, and judge what a result does and does not license.

Method literacy

See why a specific design could settle a specific question — and where it ran out.

Nature of science

Consensus can lag correct evidence. Being right is not the same as being believed.

Interdisciplinary transfer

Trace how a technique moves between fields, and build those links yourself.

Epistemic humility

Ask what every researcher got wrong. Doubt is the question that matters most.

Media literacy

Beat a claim by naming the technique carrying it, not by arguing the topic.

Honesty notice

Every line spoken here was written for this game — nothing is a real quotation. The portrait plates are interpretive illustrations, not likenesses: each is drawn in code from a handful of described features, and none is traced from a photograph. The research, methods, career histories and controversies are drawn from the public record and are meant to be accurate, but the voices are dramatisations. The manipulative claims the drones fire are invented demonstrations of named techniques, not real quotes from anyone.

Treat this as a doorway to the primary literature, never as a citable source.
